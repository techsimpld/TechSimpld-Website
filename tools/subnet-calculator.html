How to Plan Clean Subnets & VLANs for a Home Lab

In the TechSimpld lab, we separate traffic across managed switch VLANs so everyday home devices never share broadcast domains with hypervisor storage replication. Home LAN clients stay on the primary subnet, Proxmox VMs and LXC containers run on VLAN 100, and Ceph cluster replication traffic is isolated on VLAN 200.

Practical IP Allocation Rules

  1. Carve out static space before DHCP starts: Reserve .1 for your gateway, .2 through .19 for managed switches and access points, and .20 through .99 for Proxmox hosts, NAS boxes, and DNS servers. Start your DHCP pool at .100 or higher.
  2. Match VLAN IDs to the third octet: Using 10.10.100.0/24 for VLAN 100 and 10.10.200.0/24 for VLAN 200 makes firewall rules and troubleshooting logs immediately readable without opening a spreadsheet.
  3. Avoid 192.168.1.0/24 if you use VPNs: If you connect back to your home lab over WireGuard from coffee shops, hotels, or offices, those remote networks frequently use 192.168.1.0/24 or 192.168.0.0/24. Choosing a less common RFC 1918 block like 10.42.x.0/24 eliminates routing conflicts.
  4. Do not over-shrink home lab subnets: A /24 (254 usable hosts) is the sweet spot for almost every home lab VLAN. Smaller masks like /28 save no real resources in private IPv4 space and only create headaches when you add containers later.
